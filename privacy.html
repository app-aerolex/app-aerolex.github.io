AeroLex

Privacy policy

Last updated October 17, 2026

AeroLex is an aviation dictionary and study app for Android. I'm an independent developer and I build it on my own. This page explains what data the app handles and where it goes. The short version: I don't run a server, I don't have user accounts, and I don't collect analytics. The two outside services the app uses are Google AdMob (ads in the free version) and RevenueCat (to handle the Pro purchase).

What stays on your phone

Your bookmarks, notes, flashcards, quiz and game results, streak, badges and settings are saved only on your device. I never receive them. If you uninstall the app, they're deleted. The dictionary content ships inside the app, so looking up a term doesn't send anything anywhere.

Ads in the free version

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google's ads software on your phone may collect your device's advertising ID, your IP address (which gives a rough location), basic device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google's own policy explains how it uses this: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

If you're in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, or a US state with its own privacy law, the app asks for your choice before showing personalized ads. If you say no, you still get ads, but they aren't based on your data. You can change your answer at any time under Settings, then Ad privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's Android settings.

If you buy Pro, the app doesn't show or request ads.

Buying Pro

Payment goes through Google Play. I never see your card or payment details. The app uses RevenueCat to confirm the purchase and to restore it on a new phone. RevenueCat receives the purchase receipt from Google Play, a random ID it creates for your install, and basic device and app information. It doesn't receive your name or email. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

Emails you send me

If you use Send feedback, Report a problem or Report a broken link, the app opens your own email app with a message to aerolex.app@gmail.com. The message includes the app version, the content version and which entry you were looking at, so I can find the problem. You see the whole message before you send it. I use these emails only to answer you and fix the app, and I'll delete them if you ask.

Links to other websites

Source links (FAA, ICAO, EASA and others) open in your browser. Those sites have their own privacy policies.

Children

AeroLex is meant for pilots, students and aviation professionals. It isn't directed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly collect data from them.

Selling data

I don't sell your data, and I don't have any to sell.

Your choices and requests

Since your study data never leaves your phone, you control it directly. For ad data, use the in-app ad choices and your Android ad settings. If you want me to delete emails you've sent, or to ask RevenueCat to delete your purchase record, write to aerolex.app@gmail.com.

Changes

If the app starts handling data differently, I'll update this page and the date at the top before that version is released.
